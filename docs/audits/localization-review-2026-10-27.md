# GetYourSats language and terminology review

Date: 27 October 2026 (India). Review scope: wallet, marketing site, Privacy, Support, Learn, account-removal instructions, first-party links and the ten implemented language choices.

Follow-up: [the browser journey review](browser-language-journey-review-2026-10-27.md) exercises all ten language selections, reproduces the mixed-language transitions, records screenshots and supplies a prioritized implementation handoff. It distinguishes live production pages from disposable UI fixtures.

## Decision

The language menu overstates the completeness of the experience. GetYourSats has useful translations in selected flows, but it does not yet offer ten fully localized user journeys. A catalog with all required keys does not prove that the rest of the interface uses that catalog.

The privacy repair in this branch provides the complete existing notice in ten languages. This is a targeted correction, not certification of the product's entire localization or of its legal compliance. The other gaps below remain implementation work. Do not remove the existing partial-translation notices or advertise full-language support on the basis of this patch.

## Protected terminology and plain-language standard

Preserve these names in Latin script, with their official spelling: **GetYourSats, Bitcoin, Lightning, Spark, Breez**. Preserve **sat/sats, BTC, USDT, USDC, QR, PIN, KYC** where they identify units, assets or recognized abbreviations. Network and provider names retain their official names. Addresses, usernames, invoice strings, hashes, recovery words and user-entered notes must never be translated or rewritten.

“sat” is the singular unit; “sats” is plural. Keep both in Latin script. Capitalization for a heading can be handled by presentation; do not create locale-specific coin names. Do not change identifiers or SDK enum values to enforce display capitalization.

Translate ordinary interface language: wallet, send, receive, fee, balance, network, backup, recovery words and payment request. Explain a technical term the first time it matters rather than preserving every English word as supposed industry jargon. A passkey may use the platform's familiar localized name, with “passkey” in parentheses where useful. Self-custody needs a short practical explanation about control of keys and recovery responsibility.

Use short sentences, common verbs and one action per instruction. Keep a neutral, respectful tone. Avoid promotional assurances in failure, privacy and security flows. Distinguish:

- **Payment not confirmed** from **payment failed**. An unknown result requires checking status, not a translated invitation to pay again.
- **Remove from this device** from **delete server-held account information**. Neither erases public Bitcoin transactions.
- **Restore the same wallet** from **create a new wallet**. Never soften recovery warnings into optional suggestions for a manual-phrase wallet.
- **Balance**, **amount to send**, **fee** and **recipient receives**. Keep asset and network names visible.
- **Available**, **coming soon** and **unavailable for this route**. Translation must not invent feature readiness.

Examples of the intended wording: “Enter amount,” “Available balance,” “Check payment status,” “Choose the recipient’s network” and “Support cannot restore lost keys.” Keep technical infrastructure explanations in the relevant help/disclosure area without hiding essential custody or route information.

## Implementation order and acceptance criteria

1. **Privacy/terminology patch:** review the included complete translations; preserve the original policy's data categories, providers, retention rules, 5/10-minute verification expiry, public-address disclosure, KYC boundary and deletion limitations. This is not a new privacy guarantee. Have a legal reviewer validate meaning before treating it as an approved legal policy.
2. **Critical wallet journeys:** triage the static inventory and extract strings from restore, removal, confirmation, error, refunds and exits into typed catalogs. Review an immutable English source first. Add locale-aware state tests, including denial, cancellation, expiry and unknown payment outcomes. Do not alter SDK behavior while translating.
3. **Support, Learn and account removal:** one language registry and one resolver shared across page headings, prose, forms, errors and links. Translate complete content. Each selector choice must update the full page or explicitly identify unavailable content. Keep original-English text available as a deliberate reference, not a hidden substitute for missing translation.
4. **Main website and remaining wallet screens:** localize the interactive marketing states, POS, market and history controls, settings, QR/camera instructions, username/email flows and empty/error states. Do not edit user data or external invoice descriptions as if they were UI copy.
5. **Linguistic and device acceptance:** native-speaker review of each complete sensitive journey, supported region/variant decision for Portuguese, phone and tablet widths, 200% zoom, screen reader labels, text input and date/number formatting. Test browser, installed PWA and native builds separately. Third-party provider pages have their own language capabilities and cannot be promised by our selector.

The additional rendered-font pass covered all ten privacy locales. Screenshots were reviewed in Arabic, Japanese and Tamil. These results concern local fixtures, not a live production rollout.
