Wallet architecture is translated below. The detailed payment and recovery instructions on this page are currently in English.

Keep recovery words private. Support will never ask for a recovery phrase, private key, wallet PIN or passkey secret. Do not clear wallet data while a payment is unresolved or before you have checked recovery.

Check a payment

Open History in the wallet and open the payment details. Check the recipient, amount, network, time and status. A successful payment should be supported by the wallet's completed record; a QR code or sender screenshot alone does not prove that a payment arrived.

If a payment is pending or its result is unknown, do not send it again. Keep the wallet data intact, reconnect when your connection returns and check History again. If the result remains unclear, contact us with a non-secret payment identifier and the approximate time. Support can help investigate status but cannot reverse a completed payment.

Passkeys: change devices and restore your wallet

Open wallet.getyoursats.com in a full browser and choose Restore for your existing wallet. Use the original passkey and the same wallet label if asked. Start or a newly created passkey can create a different wallet.

Keep the old device, passkey and wallet data until recovery is verified. Finding a passkey or completing sign-in does not prove that the same wallet has been restored. Menu names vary by system version.

Google passkey: Android → iPhone
  1. Install and open Chrome on the iPhone. Sign in to the Google account that holds the original passkey. The Google search app alone is not Chrome or its passkey provider.
  2. On iOS 17 or later, search Settings for AutoFill & Passwords. Enable Chrome as a password/passkey provider.
  3. Open the wallet in Chrome and choose Restore. If prompted, unlock Google Password Manager with its PIN or the requested previous device screen lock. Select the existing passkey.
Google passkey: iPhone or Android → Android
  1. Use this route only if the original passkey is stored in Google Password Manager, including one created on an iPhone using Chrome.
  2. On the new Android device, sign in to the same Google account and enable Google Password Manager for passwords/passkeys in Settings. Allow sync and unlock the manager when prompted.
  3. Open the same wallet website in Chrome, choose Restore and select the original passkey. An Apple Passwords passkey does not appear just because you sign in to Google.
Apple Passwords: iPhone → iPhone or iPad
  1. Sign in to the same Apple Account on the new device. In iCloud settings, enable Passwords & Keychain sync and complete any account-recovery prompts.
  2. Enable Apple Passwords for AutoFill and check that the wallet passkey is present in Passwords.
  3. Open the original wallet website in Safari, choose Restore and select that passkey.
Apple Passwords → Android, with the iPhone nearby
  1. On Android, open the wallet and choose Restore. If offered, select the option to use a passkey from another phone or tablet.
  2. Keep both devices nearby with Bluetooth and internet enabled. Scan the displayed QR code with the iPhone holding the original passkey and approve on that iPhone.
  3. This uses the iPhone-held credential; it does not copy it to Android. Wallet support for this route remains unverified. If unavailable or unsupported, stop and preserve the working wallet.
Moving between Apple Passwords and Google Password Manager
  1. This is a platform migration option, not a verified GetYourSats recovery route. Passkey transfer may not preserve the secret needed to unlock the same wallet. Keep the original credential and device.
  2. Where both apps offer secure passkey transfer, use the source manager’s transfer/export option and select the destination manager. For an import into Google on iPhone, choose Chrome and authenticate to complete the import. A password CSV is not a passkey backup.
  3. Sync the destination manager to the new device, then verify the wallet using the checklist below. If transfer is unavailable, use a compatible existing device or an independently verified backup for this exact wallet.
No passkey appears, or sign-in is cancelled
  1. Open the exact original wallet website in a full browser, outside the Google search app or an in-app browser. Check the password manager, account, sync and AutoFill provider. Update the browser and OS.
  2. Retry Restore and complete the system prompt. A cancellation message can also mean no matching credential, timeout or an unavailable provider; it does not prove you cancelled.
  3. If the passkey appears but the wallet cannot open, the provider may lack the wallet’s required cryptographic support. Keep the original wallet; do not create a replacement passkey or clear data to fix it. Contact support.
The old phone is lost, broken or already erased
  1. First recover access to the original password-manager account and its synced passkeys on a compatible device. Apple Passwords does not automatically sync to Android. QR sign-in requires an available device holding the passkey.
  2. If that is unavailable, use only an independent recovery backup already verified for this exact wallet. A recovery phrase from a different wallet will not recover these funds.
  3. Without a compatible original passkey or a verified backup, support, email verification and a Lightning username cannot recreate the wallet keys. Do not delete any remaining wallet data.
Before you erase or replace the old device
  1. Compare the restored wallet identity, balance and payment history with the original. A wallet label alone is not proof. Stop if they differ or an unexpected empty wallet appears.
  2. Close and reopen the wallet on the new device to check access persists. Keep the old device and independent backup until recovery is verified.
  3. If any check fails, keep the original wallet intact and contact support. Do not send more funds or create a new wallet to fix recovery.

Still stuck? Contact support with device, OS and browser versions, wallet URL, error text and whether a passkey appeared. Never send recovery words, keys, PINs or passkey secrets.

Check a merchant receipt

Open Point of Sale and the order history in the receiving wallet. Match the invoice, amount and completed wallet payment before handing over goods or services. If a receipt is awaiting verification, check the matching wallet payment and contact support if the records disagree.

Merchant order details are stored locally on this device. Keep exports for your records; another device or a restored wallet may not contain those local order details. Payment history and merchant records serve different purposes.

Your Lightning address

Anonymous wallet setup does not require an email account. To choose an available name@getyoursats.com, open Settings, verify your email and follow the name-registration flow. Use the address shown after the provider confirms registration. An email or username does not grant access to the wallet's signing keys.

If an address you already shared is missing, restore the same wallet and check its address before registering a replacement. Contact support if an existing address cannot be resolved. Never send a recovery phrase to prove ownership.

How your wallet works

GetYourSats is your Bitcoin wallet. Spark provides the underlying Bitcoin balance infrastructure and Lightning connects you to compatible wallets and merchants.

Bitcoin deposits are credited to your GetYourSats balance through Spark. This is not a separate Bitcoin UTXO wallet. Confirmations and claim fees may apply.

For USDT or USDC routes, check the token, network, required deposit, costs and expected output. The quote tells you which asset your wallet will receive.

Keep the recovery method for this exact wallet safe. Support cannot recreate lost keys. Check payment history before retrying a payment with an uncertain outcome.

Your keys and recovery

GetYourSats is a wallet for Bitcoin payments with user-controlled signing keys. It uses the Breez Spark SDK and its network services. GetYourSats does not provide a custodial account that can reset your wallet keys.

The web application, your browser and the payment infrastructure remain part of the security model. Self-custody describes who controls the keys; it does not guarantee that every storage path, device or signing flow has the same protection. A passkey unlock is not by itself hardware-backed approval of a particular payment.

Keep an independent recovery backup for this wallet. Browser storage protections, spending authorization and recovery across devices require verification against the version you use. The current engineering assessment has outstanding security and recovery gates; it is not a certification for expanded real-funds adoption. Read the security guidance and product capabilities.

What helps us investigate?

Tell us your device and browser, wallet website URL, approximate time, non-sensitive error text and whether you were creating, restoring, sending or receiving. Include a payment identifier only when relevant. Payment identifiers can reveal transaction information, so share them privately. Never include recovery words, keys, passkey secrets or unredacted wallet logs. For a privacy request, read our privacy notice.
